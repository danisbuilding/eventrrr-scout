RATINGS GUIDE

One score per calendar, 1 to 5. It ranks events against each other so busy days can be cut down; it is not a grade the reader sees, and it never says "don't go".

5  Singular experience. Not to be missed.
4  Great experience. Prioritize over other things.
3  Good experience. Worth going if you're free.
2  Fine experience. Maybe go or not.
1  Mediocre experience. Lean toward skipping unless something about it is compelling.

Calibration (DP, 2026-11-03): most events are 1s and 2s; that is the score for an okay event. A 3 is already a high score. 4s are rare, 5s exceptional.

How to use it
- No score on a calendar = not on that calendar. You never have to say no; only score what belongs.
- × clears a score you set by mistake.
- Score every calendar the event is at least a 3 for. A second calendar needs a real reason, not a possible one.
- Score the experience, not the week. A 4 in a dead week is the same 4 in Día de Muertos week.
- An ongoing run, its opening night and its closing weekend are three separate entries; score each on its own.
- None of these: you looked, it fits no calendar.
- Can't tell: you looked, the listing doesn't say enough. Not a judgment on the event; it tells us what the extractor missed.
- Duplicate: same event as another row. Removes this row from the pool; its scores stay recorded.

What the curated calendar does with it (planned)
- 5s always show. 4s go in a collapsed "N more". 3s and 2s fill a day that has nothing higher. 1s never export.
- Ongoing events sit at the top of each week (weeks start Monday), ordered by closing date.
